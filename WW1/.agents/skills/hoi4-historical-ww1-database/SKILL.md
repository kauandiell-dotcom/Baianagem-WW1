## 2. Major Powers Dossiers

### A. French Republic (`FRA`)
- **Key Figures**:
  - Head of State: Raymond Poincaré (President 1913–1920, fierce patriot).
  - Prime Ministers: René Viviani (1914), Aristide Briand (1915–1917), Georges Clemenceau ("Le Tigre", 1917–1920, architect of victory).
  - Military Commanders: Joseph Joffre (Commander-in-Chief, Plan XVII), Ferdinand Foch (Supreme Allied Commander 1918), Philippe Pétain (Hero of Verdun, reformed army after 1917 mutinies), Robert Nivelle.
- **National Dilemmas**:
  - *L'Union Sacrée*: Maintaining political truce between socialists (SFIO), Catholics, and republicans.
  - *Industrial Ruin*: Loss of the industrial north and Briey iron mines in 1914.
  - *1917 French Army Mutinies*: Soldiers refuse suicidal frontal attacks after the Nivelle Offensive; Pétain restores discipline through better food, rest, and defensive tactics ("Wait for the Americans and the tanks").
- **Alternative Paths**:
  - *SFIO Peace Initiative*: Socialists break the Union Sacrée in 1917, demanding a negotiated peace without annexations.
  - *Action Française Restoration*: Charles Maurras and nationalist reactionaries overthrow the Third Republic, restoring an Orléanist monarchy.
  - *Foch Military Directorate*: Foch establishes military rule to dictate terms and break Germany into Rhine river buffer states.

---

### B. Russian Empire / Soviet Russia (`RUS` / `SOV`)
- **Key Figures**:
  - Head of State: Tsar Nicholas II (1894–1917), Grand Duke Michael, Alexander Kerensky (Provisional Govt), Vladimir Lenin & Leon Trotsky (Bolsheviks).
  - Prime Ministers: Pyotr Stolypin (Agrarian reformer, assassinated Sept 1911), Ivan Goremykin, Boris Stürmer.
  - Generals: Grand Duke Nicholas Nikolaevich, Alexei Brusilov, Alexander Samsonov, Paul von Rennenkampf, Anton Denikin, Lavr Kornilov.
- **National Dilemmas**:
  - *Shell Hunger (1915)*: Acute shortage of artillery ammunition and rifles causing catastrophic retreat.
  - *St. Petersburg Food Riots*: Transport collapse and bread shortages spark the February 1917 Revolution.
  - *Order No. 1*: Petrograd Soviet abolishes officer authority, causing total army disintegration.
- **Alternative Paths**:
  - *Stolypin Survives*: Agrarian reforms complete, creating a prosperous peasant class that resists radical Bolshevism.
  - *Kornilov Military Coup (Aug 1917)*: General Kornilov crushes the Petrograd Soviet, instituting a military dictatorship to fight to the end.
  - *Tsarist Constitutional Compromise*: Nicholas II grants true legislative power to the Duma under Progressive Bloc leadership, averting the revolution.

### C. British Empire & Great Britain (`ENG`)
- **Key Figures**:
  - Prime Ministers: H. H. Asquith (Liberal, 1908–1916), David Lloyd George (War Cabinet, 1916–1922).
  - Ministers & Leaders: Winston Churchill (First Lord of Admiralty until Gallipoli), Lord Kitchener (Secretary of State for War), Edward Grey (Foreign Secretary).
  - Military Commanders: Field Marshal Douglas Haig (Commander of BEF 1915–1918), Sir John French, Admiral John Jellicoe, Admiral David Beatty.
- **National Dilemmas**:
  - *Irish Home Rule & 1916 Easter Rising*: Domestic conflict in Ireland threatens Imperial unity.
  - *The Shell Crisis of 1915*: Scandals over explosive artillery supply topples Asquith's government.
  - *Naval Starvation Blockade*: Royal Navy blockades the North Sea, starving Central Powers but straining relations with neutral USA.
- **Alternative Paths**:
  - *Early Asquith Negotiated Peace (1916)*: Britain seeks status quo ante bellum before financial exhaustion.
  - *Splendid Isolation Reinforced*: Britain refuses to guarantee Belgium in 1914, leaving France to fight alone.
  - *Imperial Federation*: Granting equal Westminster parliamentary seats to Canada, Australia, and South Africa.

---

### D. Austro-Hungarian Empire (`AUS`)
- **Key Figures**:
  - Emperors: Franz Joseph I (1848–1916), Karl I (Blessed Karl, 1916–1918).
  - Politicians: Count Leopold Berchtold (Foreign Minister 1914), Count István Tisza (Prime Minister of Hungary), Count Ottokar Czernin.
  - Generals: Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf (Chief of Staff), Svetozar Boroević ("Lion of the Isonzo"), Archduke Friedrich.
- **National Dilemmas**:
  - *Dualism vs Ethnic Collapse*: Compromise of 1867 privileged Germans and Hungarians while alienating Czechs, South Slavs, Poles, and Romanians.
  - *Multi-Front Strain*: Fighting simultaneous wars in Galicia (vs Russia), the Balkans (vs Serbia/Romania), and the Alps (vs Italy).
  - *Sixtus Affair (1917)*: Emperor Karl's secret peace overtures to France exposed by Clemenceau, alienating Germany.
- **Alternative Paths**:
  - *Trialism (United Kingdom of Greater Austria)*: Reorganizing the empire into a federation of German, Hungarian, and Slavic kingdoms.
  - *Austro-Marxist Federation*: Danubian democratic socialist confederation.
  - *German Annexation (Großdeutschland)*: Collapse of the Dual Monarchy leads to Austrian German provinces integrating into Germany.

---

### E. Ottoman Empire (`OTT`)
- **Key Figures**:
  - The Three Pashas: Enver Pasha (Minister of War), Talaat Pasha (Minister of Interior), Djemal Pasha (Minister of Navy).
  - Sultans: Mehmed V Reşâd (1909–1918), Mehmed VI Vahideddin (1918–1922).
  - Commanders: Mustafa Kemal (Hero of Gallipoli), Otto Liman von Sanders, Fevzi Çakmak.
- **National Dilemmas**:
  - *Financial Bankruptcy & Capitulations*: Foreign debt control over Ottoman economy.
  - *Caucasus & Mesopotamian Fronts*: Severe supply deficiencies across mountains and deserts.
  - *The Arab Revolt (1916)*: Sharif Hussein and British forces sever the Levant and Hejaz.
- **Alternative Paths**:
  - *Entente Alignment (1914)*: Enver's pro-German faction fails; Ottomans remain neutral or join Entente in exchange for Aegean islands.
  - *Kemalist Secular Republic Early*: Mustafa Kemal and progressive officers seize power during the Gallipoli glory.
  - *Pan-Turanian Empire*: Victorious conquest of the Caucasus and Central Asian Turkic peoples following Russian collapse.

---

### F. Kingdom of Italy (`ITA`)
- **Key Figures**:
  - Leaders: Antonio Salandra (PM 1914–1916), Paolo Boselli (1916–1917), Vittorio Emanuele Orlando (PM 1917–1919), King Victor Emmanuel III.
  - Military: Luigi Cadorna (Chief of Staff 1914–1917, decimated troops for minor gains), Armando Diaz (rebuilt army after Caporetto).
- **National Dilemmas**:
  - *Sacred Egoism & Treaty of London*: Choosing whether to join Central Powers, stay neutral, or join Entente for Trentino, Trieste, and Dalmatia.
  - *The Isonzo Grinder*: 11 bloody, inconclusive battles in the karst mountains.
  - *Caporetto Disaster (1917)*: Austro-German stormtroopers shatter Italian lines, capturing 300,000 prisoners.
- **Alternative Paths**:
  - *Honor the Triple Alliance*: Italy honors its 1882 alliance with Germany and Austria-Hungary, invading southern France.
  - *Armed Neutrality (Giolittian Path)*: Bargaining with Austria-Hungary for Trentino without entering the bloodbath.
  - *Mussolini / Nationalist Early March*: Radical interventionists launch an early nationalist revolution in 1917.

---

### G. United States of America (`USA`)
- **Key Figures**:
  - President: Woodrow Wilson (1913–1921, "He Kept Us Out of War" -> Fourteen Points).
  - Politicians: Theodore Roosevelt (Preparedness movement leader), Henry Cabot Lodge (Republican Senate leader).
  - Military: General John J. Pershing ("Black Jack", commander of the American Expeditionary Forces).
- **National Dilemmas**:
  - *Strict Neutrality vs Entente Trade*: American banks finance Allied war supplies while German U-boats target merchant vessels.
  - *Zimmermann Telegram (Feb 1917)*: German proposal for Mexican invasion of Texas, Arizona, and New Mexico inflames public opinion.
  - *AEF Independence*: Pershing insists American troops fight as an independent army rather than piecemeal replacements in French/British divisions.
- **Alternative Paths**:
  - *Teddy Roosevelt 1916 Victory*: Roosevelt wins 1916 presidential election and enters the war by early 1917 with full mobilization.
  - *Strict Embargo / Complete Isolation*: Wilson bans all loans and munitions exports to Europe, preserving absolute neutrality.
  - *War on Britain (Free Seas Crisis)*: Escalation against the British naval blockade over violation of American neutral trading rights.
